Before You Begin

Prevent the behavior before you correct it.

Most owners try to correct bad behavior after it happens. Professional trainers focus on preventing unwanted behavior before it starts.

These five steps take about 15 minutes a day and create the foundation for better listening, calmer behavior, and a stronger relationship.

1

Exercise Before You Expect Excellence

A dog with excess energy struggles to concentrate. Before asking your dog to sit, stay, or walk politely, prepare the brain and body.

  • Take a brisk 10-20 minute walk.
  • Play fetch or structured movement games.
  • Practice short recall games with focus.
Trainer tip: Exercise is not the reward. It prepares the brain for learning.

2

Reward the Behavior You Want

Dogs repeat what gets rewarded. Instead of waiting for mistakes, reward eye contact, calm body language, and checking in during daily life.

  • Mark and reward quiet attention.
  • Praise calm decisions before the dog escalates.
  • Give fewer corrections by creating more successful moments.
3

Wait for the First Calm Moment

Do not rush the command. Pause until your dog gives you even a small moment of attention. Then guide the next behavior clearly.

  • Ask once when possible.
  • Stay calm and consistent.
  • Teach your dog that patience opens the door to success.

4

Consistency Beats Intensity

Long, exhausting sessions are not required. Short, clear repetitions done every day create the patterns your dog can actually understand.

  • Practice 3-5 minutes in the morning.
  • Add a short afternoon or evening win.
  • Use the same words, tone, timing, and expectations.
5

End Every Session With Success

Never stop immediately after confusion or frustration. Finish with something your dog knows well so the session ends with confidence.

  • Use a known command like sit, down, touch, or place.
  • Celebrate the win and end cleanly.
  • Help your dog want to come back and learn again.
